A8. INFLAMMATION

Inflammation is the most general non-specific defense mechanism of the tissues against any tissue-damaging factor (noxa), at the same time – besides fever – it is a pertinent feature of infections.

A8.1. EVOKING FACTORS OF INFLAMMATION

The aim of effector mechanisms of inflammation is to defend the tissues, organs and the whole body – partly by eliminating the damaging factor (e.g.in infection), partly by eliminating the consequences of tissue injury (e.g. post-ischemic inflammation) – from various damages caused by the tissue injury. The effector mechanisms may be of cell-mediated or humoral character. The inflammatory reactions basically (but not exclusively) affect both the innate and the adaptive immune response. However, the inflammation and the function of immune cells are not equal (e.g. a vascular endothelial cell also exhibits inflammatory type reactions). The acute inflammation is performed according to a stereotype scheme, what does not basically depend on the type of the evoking noxa. The innate immune reaction is activated in the first minutes of the inflammation and – in fortunate cases – it ceases within hours (or a few days), and it is followed by complete tissue reparation. In contrast, chronic inflammation may las for weeks, months, or even years, this type of immunity would be better avoided. However, the acute inflammation may recur, may go over to chronic form, while chronic inflammations may improve spontaneously or may produce acute exacerbations.

EXOGENOUS tissue-damaging noxious agents

Mechanical: physical trauma (wound), foreign body incorporation, surgery, implants

Physical: burns, freezing, heat, UV-, electromagnetic-, ionizing- radiation

Chemical: acids, bases, organic solvents, travel-, industrial-, and agricultural-contamination, fito-alkaloides (e.g. nettle, “poison ivy”, parsnip)

Biological: LPS (endotoxin), various components and agents of bacteria, viruses, fungi, rickettsia, protozoons, helminthes, snakes, insects, spiders, jelly-fish, etc. Fito-allergens (ragweed).

ENDOGENOUS tissue-damaging processes

Circulatory disorders: ischemia, reperfusion, (infarceration)

Trigger molecule: Macromolecule: autoimmunity, immune-complex disease

Micromolecule: pl. urate crystals activate plasma contact-system

Endogenous toxins: bilirubin, bile salts, urea, ammonia

Activation of proteases: acute pancreatitis, DIC, ARDS

Lack of protease inhibitors:

Congenital: arthritis, angioneurotic edema, emphysema

Acquired: ARDS, septic shock

According to our present knowledge the response of the body to pathogens and/or tissue damage three members of the so-called pattern recognition receptor (PRR) – expressed on immune cells – may initiate and direct the activation of the innate immune response. These are able to recognize (and bind) conserved sequences of microbial (but not of vertebrates) elements (PAMP: pathogen/microbe associated molecular pattern), and also those cellular elements that are released from cell necrosis and which signalize the danger of tissue injury (DAMP: danger associated molecular pattern).

Some examples for the ligands of the three receptor families:

1.  TOLL-LIKE RECEPTORS (TLR 1-10);

TLR-4: LPS (endotoxin); TLR-2: Gram-positive/negative and mycobacterial lipoproteins, Gram-positive peptidoglycan lipoteichoic acid and fungal zymosan, HMGB-1; TLR-4 sentinel: DAMP (pl. glycosaminoglycan); TLR-9 (intracellular): bacterial CpG DNS

2. NOD RECEPTORS (nucleotide-oligomerization domain leucine-rich repeat: NOD-LRR)     These bind peptidoglycan fragments. NOD1: diamino pimelate; NOD2: muramyl dipeptide

3. RLH-s (retinoic-acid-inducible gene I-like helicases)

Recognition of viral nucleic acid samples with consequent interferon production.

Certain soluble molecules also belong to the PRR group, e.g. mannose-binding lectins, ficolins and collectins, and the immunoglobulin proteins belonging to the RAGE (receptor for advanced glycation end-products) receptor family, which also bind the HMGB-1.

The DAMP molecules – heat shock proteins, fibrinogen, fibronectin, hyaluran, biglycans and the HMGB-1 (high mobility group box-1) proteins – are recognized partly also by the TLR receptors, mainly the TLR4. The expression of TLR is enhanced and sensitize to endotoxin the TLR signal transmission, i.e. presence of tissue injury (trauma, ischemia) magnifies the inflammatory reaction to infection.

The TLR signaling activates transcription factors as e.g. the interferon regulatory factors, the phosphoinositol 3-kinase (PI3K)/Act, activator protein-1, and most importantly the nuclear factor-kappa B-t (NF-κB), which – after reaching into the nucleus from the plasma, after proteosomal (signalosoma) degradation of I-kappa B (I-κB) – ignites the expression of several proinflammatory protein, including many cytokines and chemokines, that are important in the communication between the immunocytes. Activation of NF-κB is also under dynamic redox control by a I-κB glutaredoxin regulated S-glutathionylation. This function plays a role in the NF-κB activation in the course of tissue ischemia/hypoxia – reperfusion. The activated cells produce other inflammatory mediators, too (arachidonic acid derivatives, bradykinin, etc.), which play a role e.g. in evoking the vascular phenomena and pain.

The PRR-s regulate the activation of the so-called “inflammosome” protein complex. In this, apart from others, the caspase-1 and -5 proteases, the ASC (apoptosis-associated speck-like protein) and the NOD-LRR proteins also participate. The ASC besides constructing the inflammosome also induces the caspase-1 activation. Certain (1,4,5,12) caspase cysteine proteases (1-12) some play a role in the process of inflammation, others in the apoptosis. A substrate of the latter ones, e.g. caspase-activated DN-ase is responsible for the characteristic DNA fragmentation in apoptosis. The caspase-1 activated in inflammosome splits the pro-Interleukin-1β, producing the active IL-1 β. It may be of interest that the ASC protein also acts on the signalosome-NF-κB pathway.

The inflammosome is also the initiator of programmed cell death by the „pyroptotic” mechanism. In contrast to apoptosis in pyroptosis the plasma-membrane gets broken and the DAMP molecules, like ATP, DNA or ASC oligomers, and cytokines are released into the extracellular space – this induced further immune-cell infiltration and further enhancement of the tissue inflammatory reaction.  In contrast, during apoptosis the membrane-bound apoptotic bodies are phagocytosed without important inflammatory reaction (”non-phlogistic” elimination).

A8.2. ACUTE INFLAMMATION

A8.2.1. MAIN SYMPTOMS OF ACUTE INFLAMMATION

From Celsus: calor (warm)-rubor (red)-tumor (swollen)-dolor (pain) -functio laesa (dysfunction)

The first 4 symptoms have been known for centuries since Celsus, the last one was described by Virchow in 1858. Metchnikov in 1892 described the microscopic features of inflammation: cellular infiltration, phagocytosis.

According to present views the fundamental features of the cellular response are: leukocyte adhesion, emigration (chemotaxis), receptorial-recognition, phagocytosis (ingestion), digestion in phagosome → degranulation → free radical production during „oxidative burst”.

It is characteristic for the acute inflammation, that after the demonstrative local and general symptoms are over, in most cases restitutio ad integrum is possible.

2. Cellular infiltration

Leukocytes emigrate from the vascular compartment at the capillaries or better the venules → extravasation of cells (through basement membrane). First the neutrophils, then the monocytes (activation: macrophage), later the lymphocytes. The neutrophils soon disappear, later the lymphocytes, too. Macrophages remain for longest period permanently, later these participate in the reparation, too.

On the location of inflammation, upon the released mediators the endothelial cells of the surrounding vessels (mainly venulas) translocate P-selectin from their Weber-Palade granulas to the luminal membrane surface, to which the PSGL-1 (P-selectin ligand) adhesion molecules of the neutrophil granulocytes can bind with great affinity. Further, the inflammatory endothelium also expresses E-selectin, neutrophil L-selectin ligands and neutrophil activating agents (e.g. PAF, IL-8). Binding of neutrophils is followed by the ”rolling” phase, when the surface appearance of secretory vesicula increasing number of β2-integrin CD11b/CD18 complex appears on the membrane. The β2-integrin binds to the ICAM-1 adhesion molecules of the endothelium, leading to transmigration of neutrophils. In the course of this, from the gelatinase granula of the endothelial collagenolytic metalloproteases are released, helping extravasation by degradation of basalmembrane. For movement of neutrophils in the interstititum by partial degranulation of their azurophilic degranulation release collagenase, serine-proteases, and also matrix receptors. Upon the phagocytes (neutrophil and macrophage) meet with the pathogenic agent, from their specific granula antibacterial peptides and proteolytic and oxidative enzymes are released from their specific secretory granula. The anti-bacterial α- and β-defensins, cathelicidines, lactoferrin, lysozyme damage the membrane pf the pathogen. Lactoferrin and other peptides bind the iron. The NADPH oxidase produces superoxide anion, which in watery milieu spontaneously or upon SOD effect dismutase to hydrogen peroxidase, what is transformed in the presence of myeloperoxidase (MOP) and reactive Cl-  ion it transforms it to reactive hypochloric acid. Source of superoxide may be the activity of xanthine oxidase, particularly in the course of ischemia-reperfusion, but it may be activated by the effect of TNF-αand endotoxin. The superoxide with reaction of nitrogen monoxide (NO•) reacts with peroxynitrite (ONOO-•) and produces free radical, which, amongst others, damages DNA and activates the poly adenine-dinucleotide phosphate-ribose polymerase (PARP), which is one of the main mediators of organ dysfunctions.

3. Tissue-necrosis, then reparation

The necrotized debris (e.g. the produced pus) is removed by phagocytes. According to the type of tissue and the noxa, the morphological types may be different: coagulative-, colliquative-, fibrinoid-, etc. necrosis.

Nota Bene: The necrotizing tissue damage is both an eliciting factor and a consequence of inflammation. Example may be for the latter necrotizing enterocolitis, or pancreatitis, or fasciitis necrotica (Plaut-Vincent angina). The classic form of the former is the inflammatory reaction to tissue damage because of an infarcered myocardium, which functions as demarcation zone for the ischemically necrotized tissues, then serves in removal of debris, finally in reparative fibrotic transformation (scar formation) of the infarcered area. For further details, see chapter on coronary blood flow (ch. 2.6.1.). Other tissue damage may elicit similar reaction, even with cell necrosis: burns, freezing, irradiation, cell toxins (e.g. cyanide), etc.

Factors in cellular necrosis:

Incapability of maintaining membrane potential → Na+ and Ca++-influx → cell and mitochondrial swelling → mitochondrial membrane fragmentation → insufficiency of energy utilization and ATP production → free radicals from the injured electron-transport chain → energy deficit, impairment of regeneration system against free radicals (glutathione, vitamin-E, vitamin-C, SOD, catalase) → destructive IC enzymes activated (phospholipase, xanthine oxidase) → reactive products → free radical induced injury of cell structure (cytoskeleton, organellar membranes) → cell death → inflammatory reaction → phagocytosis → breakdown and reconstruction of EC matrix (role of matrix metalloproteases: MMP-s) → fibrotic transformation.

Components released from necrotizing cells provide a DAMP signal (e.g. HMGB-1) for immune cells, thereby enhancing their phagocytic activity. In contrast, the phagocytic activity of the neutrophils elicits proapoptotic changes in the cell.  It had been observed already by Metchnikoff that the macrophages gobble up the ”microphages” in the process of resolution of the inflammation. A critical point of the inflammation that it should remain barriered in time and space, and its permanent escalation should not endanger the survival of the organism. In better cases this can be reached by elimination or neutralization of the agent that elicited the inflammation. If this does not succeed (or only partly succeeds), the inflammation may turn into a chronic form, the actual picture of which – in contrast to the acute inflammation – depends on the characteristics of the inflammatory agent.

If the localization of inflammation is not successful, systemic inflammation (SIRS), or – in case of infection – septic state develops (ch. A10.3.). A collaboration between hemostasis and inflammation results in demarcation of inflamed region from the intact one, what is part of a basic survival strategy of the organism. The connection is bidirectional: the inflammation activates the coagulation cascade, while initiation of the coagulation is an inflammatory stimulus. Binding of serine-protease coagulation factors (e.g. thrombin) proteinase-activated receptors (PAR 1-4) leads to expression of endothelin P-selectin, adhesion molecules and cytokines, what results in disorder of microcirculation in the region of inflammation (e.g. vascular occlusion > necrosis).

With elimination of the evoking stimulus the further release of inflammatory mediators (eicosanoids, cytokines, chemokines, etc.) ceases, they disappear due to their fast catabolism, and the granulocyte infiltration stops. For attraction of macrophages a shift of chemokines may be responsible, which shift is due to granulocyte-produced soluble IL-6 Receptor and the IL-6 binding. The CXC chemokine production induced by proinflammatory IL-1 and TNFα – which draws the neutrophils – decreases, while the production of CC chemokine macrophage chemoattractant 1 increases. The chemotaxis of granulocytes is also inhibited by the simultaneously produced eicosanoid derivative lipoxins (LX). A small fraction of the granulocytes and lymphocytes of the inflammatory area may recirculate, but typically these are taken up by macrophages (after apoptosis or necrosis) and leave the area by the lymphatic vessels. The extrinsic pathway of the granulocyte apoptosis is induced by activation of the TNF receptor-family, what activates the apoptotic caspases. The process is not irreversible, and under certain circumstances the cell may survive. Necrosis of the granulocytes would lead to release of their intracellular content, what would mean an enhancing stimulus (DAMP) of the inflammatory process. However, the inflammation is decreased by the phagocytosis of granulocytes (which maintain membrane integrity during apoptosis) by macrophages, since in those cells the antiinflammatory TGF-β1 and PGE2 are released, while the proinflammatory IL-8, TNF and TXA2 production decreases. This means the ”non-phlogistic” elimination of granulocytes.

Already in the early phase of granulocyte apoptosis – probably due to rise in intracellular Ca++ level, and to nitrosylation of its sulfhydride groups, – the membrane aminophospholipid translocase is inhibited, while the lipid scramblase is activated; this leads to elimination of physiological membrane-asymmetria, thereby phosphatidyl serine (PS) becomes expressed on the outer surface of the membrane. This process is facilitated by ABCA1 (ATP binding cassette 1) transport protein, which plays an indispensable role in gobbling the cell. The PS means an ”eat me” signal for PS-receptor containing macrophages and this is the main mediator of the release of antiinflammatory cytokines. In the recognition of apoptotic cells also participate the so-called „scavenger receptors (SR-A, SR-B1, oxLDLR-1, CD36) and the mannose receptor, too. Apart from promoting granulocyte apoptosis, it is also important that an element of the antiinflammatory effect of glucocorticoids may be that on the surface of the apoptotic cell, beside PS, glucocorticoid-induced annexin-1 (lipocortin) also facilitates gobbling of the cell.

In case the complete removal of apoptotic cells is imperfect, their secondary necrosis produces a new inflammatory stimulus, or means autoantigen presentation and induction of an autoimmune process.

5. Systemic features of inflammation:

I. Fever: shift of the “set point” of the central thermoregulatory center upon the effect of pyrogenic substances (ch. 8.5.3.)

II. Increased leukocyte number, high antibody production, increased amount of immunoglobulins, during acute-phase response the level of certain acute phase proteins (e.g. CRP, amyloid A) is elevated, while the production of albumin and transferrin decreases. Alteration in the composition of plasma proteins is shown non-specifically by the erythrocyte sedimentation rate what is commonly used in the diagnostics of inflammation.

III. Hemostasis is altered (in sepsis this may lead to DIC, ch. 4.4.3.2.)

IV. Even acute inflammation brings systemic symptoms: fever – malaise – weakness – exhaustion – depression. These are not only by-products of muscle weakness, energy deficiency, but form a controlled and coordinated complex, i.e. components of the acute phase response.

Evoking factors: infection, injury, inflammation, immune-activation

Components: fever, anorexia, sleepiness, decreased physical activity, lack of social concern, avoiding society, depression, lethargy.

"Aims", pathophysiological role: to overcome infection, to enhance immunity, to save energy, to prevent too severe inflammation.

Mediators: Inflammatory mediatory-processes from the periphery to the central nervous system: 1.: direct (limited, due to blood-brain-barrier) 2.: neural communication (afferent and efferent vagus) 3.: humoral communication: by endothelial mechanisms through the circumvetricular organs.

Possible clinical syndromes of sickness behavior: Depression (as illness), Chronic exhaustion syndrome, Fibromyalgia syndrome, SIRS: Systemic Inflammatory Response Syndrome (ch. A10.).

A8.3. CHRONIC INFLAMMATION

A8.3.1. CHARACTERISTICS OF CHRONIC INFLAMMATION

The chronic inflammation is maintained by continuous presence of the noxa (foreign body, chemical exposition, etc.), or by protracted immune (or autoimmune) reaction evoked by an initial endogenous/exogenous noxa. The role of adaptive immune response is more important in the majority of the chronic inflammations than in acute ones (in acute cases the exception is the anaphylactic reaction: in the organism once exposed to an allergen IgE antibodies are produced, their presence on the surface of mastocytes and basophiles is important for degranulation upon the repeated exposure to the allergen).

In chronic inflammation the vascular phenomena are moderate, while the cellular infiltration and the fibrotic transformation of the extracellular matrix is more pronounced, and this may cause functional disorder besides the tissue injury. It is still possible that a low-flame chronic inflammation eventually flares up and shows the classic picture of an acute inflammation (calor, rubor, tumor, dolor). The process may be typical e.g. in silicosis: the phagocytes are unable to eliminate the quarz-pieces that are released again and again, and as chronic noxa, they cause fibrosis, tissue injury, functional disorders, and also tendency for acute inflammatory diseases.

A8.3.2. SPECIFIC FEATURES OF CHRONIC INFLAMMATION

Chronic inflammation may be caused by continuous presence of a noxa (foreign bodies, chemical exposition, etc.), or by a protracted immune reaction evoked by an initial to (endogenous or exogenous) noxa.

It is characteristic for chronic inflammation that the vascular changes are more moderate, while the cellular infiltration and the fibrotic transformation of the extracellular matrix are more pronounced, altogether producing lasting tissue damage and abnormalities of the function of the tissue/organ. This does not exclude the possibility that a chronic inflammation going on a low level, occasionally produces acute exacerbations (“Schub”) with the classic features (calor, rubor, tumor, dolor) of acute inflammation.

Usual clinical manifestations of chronic inflammation:

Atopic (allergic) states: bronchial asthma, “hey-fever” (ragweed), eczema (atopic dermatitis)

Autoimmune diseases: rheumatoid arthritis, SLE (systemic lupus erythematodes), PCP (polyarthritis chronica progressiva), psoriasis, etc.

Chronic processes in which inflammation has a key, but not striking role:

Obesity: Many pro-inflammatory cytokines (e.g. TNF-() get into the circulation.

Diabetes: The glycation end-products (AGE) activate the monocytes through “scavenger receptors”.

Metabolic syndrome: Both the oxidated LDL and the angiotensin initiate inflammatory reactions.

Atherosclerosis: Besides oxidated LDL and macrophages, the role of matrix metalloproteases (MMP) is important in the formation of plaque and its disruption.

Aging: In the process of aging the cumulative injuring effect of free radicals is unquestionable, which substances in turn initiate chronic inflammation. This is expressed by the expression of “inflammageing”. Inflammation has a prominent role in the development of various forms of old-age dementia: Alzheimer-, Parkinson- and senile dementia.

NB: The inflammatory infiltration is a further source of free radicals, i.e. the chronic inflammation becomes a self-maintaining process.
