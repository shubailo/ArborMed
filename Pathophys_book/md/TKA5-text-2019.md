A5. HEPATOTOXINS AND ALCOHOL

In detoxification of various substances, the liver plays an eminent role. Fat-soluble toxins can be eliminated exclusively by the liver. The most common toxin that damages the liver is alcohol. Alcohol and its metabolites affect several organ functions.

A5.1. HEPATOTOXINS

Many substances may evoke hepatic injury by inhalation, parenteral or by oral means. There are hepatotoxic industrial toxins (e.g. CCl4, yellow phosphor and trichloroethylene), mushroom toxins, (e.g. amatoxin of a death cup causes fulminant liver damage and atrophia hepatis flava), alcohol and many pharmacological agents. Some of these agents can be applied only in hospitals, while others must adhere to strict prescription rules regarding administration, however, there are hepatotoxins available in the form of “over-the-counter” medicaments, too. There are strongly toxic substances among the herbal medicines of the natural medicine, which are involved in the detoxification by the liver (e.g. common comfrey, blue skullcap, kava-pepper; N.B.: the classic toxins of the middle ages were often simply regarded as ”herbal extracts of natural origin” such as the digitalis of foxglove, or atropine, and hyosciamine extracts from belladonna).

Two mechanisms can be differentiated regarding the hepatotoxic effect. Some substances or their toxic derivatives( exert a direct toxic effect, their effect appears relatively quickly, sometimes within a few hours, but at least within 24-48 hours in a certain (reliably predictable) percentage of the affected patients. The effect is dose-dependent, its mechanism is well-defined, characteristic for the given substance, e.g. hepatocellular necrosis (CCl4, Fe, INH = isonicotinic acid-hydrazide = antituberculotic drug), steatosis (steroid treatment, intravenous tetracycline, valproate), cholestasis (erythromycin, rifampicin, chlorpromazine). In contrast, the effect of the drugs causing idiosynchrasic reaction is incalculable, they cause hepatic damage only among particularly sensitive individuals, there is no dose-response relationship, the latency of the drug-effect is variable, and the mechanism in some cases is based on immune reaction, yet it is mostly unknown. It occurs more frequently among women above the age 50-y.

Some hepatotoxic substances and the mechanisms of injury

Industrial toxin

CCl4 – hepatocellular necrosis, through inhibition of glycolysis, in hepatocytes by decreasing the ATP synthesis.

Yellow phosphor – causes hepatocellular necrosis.

Mushroom toxins

Amatoxin – hemorrhagic hepatocellular necrosis by inhibition of mRNS synthesis.

Aflatoxin – toxin of fungi found on cereals and red pepper (at least 20 forms are known), it causes hepatic injury already in a dose of 10 g/kg (the lethal dose in adults is below 10 mg/kg) – it is strongly carcinogenic in a small dose, particularly in the case of repeated exposition. It has a role in the development of kwashiorkor disease.

Alcohol (ch. A5.2. and ch. 7.6.1.3.)

Medicaments

Acetaminophen (paracetamol: it is marketed using a variety of names and is an over-the-counter drug) – centrilobular hepatocellular necrosis due to exhaustion of glutathione store (it is needed for neutralizing a toxic Phase I substance), and the consequent damage due to free radicals.

In attempted suicide, or in accidental cases involving small children, an intake of 10-15 g paracetamol in severe cases after 4-12-h (in mild cases only 2-5 days) results in nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, followed by the onset of abdominal pain and circulatory shock. The lethal dose is about 25 g, in alcoholics, due to the enhanced activity of the P450 system, 2 g is considered toxic. The antidote is acetyl-cysteine, and if treatment is initiated within the first 10-12-h, it has a preventive effect.

INH (Isoniazid - antituberculotic) – toxic and idiosynchrasic effects

In nearly 20% of the treated patients, the elevation of hepatic enzymes can be observed in the first few weeks. Real liver damage develops in about 1% of patients, and is potentially lethal. With increasing age (>35 years), the risk increases. Late hepatic damage may appear even 1-year following the treatment, when it is obviously not considered. The rifampicin itself is hepatotoxic, and the combination of the two drugs increases the risk of hepatic damage.

Tetracycline – in a dose above 1.5 g/day causes steatosis of the liver.

Statins (HMG-CoA-reductase inhibitors) – idiosynchrasic effect

Steroids – primarily cholestasis may develop and inflammation is less characteristic

Troglitazone (PPAR- agonist) – idiosynchrasic effect: some patients required a liver transplant (at present, it is withdrawn)

Trovafloxacin (quinolone) antibiotic – idiosynchrasic effect

Halothane – its toxic metabolites cause cellular hypoxia, and non-specific immune-reaction plays a role in the development of febrile hepatitis-like alterations

A5.2. PATHOPHYSIOLOGY OF ALCOHOL EFFECTS

Strikingly, ethyl-alcohol (later on, alcohol) is the second most commonly used form of psychoactive stimulant worldwide. Caffeine is considered the first. In Hungary, alcohol consumption is very high. As an energy source, 1 g alcohol provides 30 kJ energy, and regarding its metabolism, a source in B1 vitamin is needed, similarly as in the case with carbohydrates. Alcohol can also be considered as an appetizer substance, enhancer of gastric acid secretion, “roborant” pharmacon, sedative drug, which even in small doses may have toxic effects upon many cell types (liver, pancreas, nerves), and in large concentrations on any tissue.

In our culture, 90% of the general public considered as an adult, consumes alcohol (max. 10 % is abstinent). Amongst the population, to varying degrees, there is a transient problem of alcohol consumption, in which 10% of men and 3-5% of women may become alcoholic.

If and once alcoholism is regarded as a chronic disease, in its development genetic and environmental factors may be demonstrated.

A5.2.1. ETIOLOGICAL FACTORS OF ALCOHOLISM

The role of genetic factors is emphasized regarding the various studies made on human twins in which the concordance in homozygotes is 74%, and in heterozygotes it is 36%. The importance of genetic factors also underlines the special neurotransmitter pattern associated in genetically alcohol-preferring rats: in the nucleus accumbens, higher dopamine, lower serotonin higher GABA levels and an increased number of GABA-erg synapses were found. Other regions of the brain were characterized by low serotonin levels. These animals e.g. in operant behavioral tests in which the specimens were willing work for the alcohol: by pressing a pedal in behavioral experiments through an oral or venous cannula, they obtained alcohol (administered by the venous route means that this was not due to the gustatory value of the alcohol).

The role of environmental factors is demonstrated by the experiment in which genetically non-alcohol-preferring rats expressed a preference upon an environmental effect (e.g. first glucose was mixed with the alcohol-containing solution), in other experiments related to alcohol preferring male rats, the preference decreased after they were treated with estrogen. The importance of environment can be observed also in human practice, in which it is highly regarded and well-known, of course, the effect of the “wrong company”. The alcohol preference is also more frequent in men, and it can be decreased by estrogen.

A5.2.2. ALCOHOL AND ITS AFFECTS IN THE BODY

The oral intake of alcohol is followed by absorption in the stomach and the often referred to, ”first pass” metabolism. This alcohol-dehydrogenase (ADH) mediated mechanism is less effective in women, and may explain their higher sensitivity towards alcohol. During fasting or using H2 receptor antagonist cimetidine, this process is weaker. The absorption of alcohol is finished in the small bowel, then it is metabolized in the liver, pancreas, and inevitably, alcohol and its metabolites are excreted by the kidney. The speed of alcohol metabolism is relatively standard (8-10 g/h), and in still healthy regular consumers, it may reach 12-16 g/h-t, and, in the case of liver disease, it is low. Notably, alcohol can also appear in the exhaled air.

CONSEQUENCES OF THE ENHANCED ACTIVITY OF THE ADH-PATHWAY:

- The elevation of the NADH/NAD ratio leads to a redox state corresponding to hypoxia, with many consequences of this, e.g. lactate-acidosis, enhanced xanthine-oxidase activity (the NADH promotes the ferritin Fe3+ ( Fe2+ transformation and the xanthine-dehydrogenase – xanthine-oxidase transformation) and consequently, risks developing hyperuricemia, free radical production, and enhanced lipid peroxidation (ch. 9.1.3.).

- Acetaldehyde (toxin) production

Metabolic effects

Carbohydrate

Upon NAD depletion, lactate is produced from the pyruvic acid of glycolytic pathway.

The NADH accumulation inhibits the gluconeogenesis (the pyruvate-oxaloacetate substrate levels are also smaller), therefore in starvation (following the exhaustion of glycogen stores) the alcohol consumption leads to hypoglycemia. In well-fed individuals, the acute alcohol consumption-induced catecholamine secretion may even evoke hyperglycemia.

Lipid

At the periphery, the fat mobilization increases, and in the background stands the alcohol-induced rise in catecholamine levels, or an eventual fasting state, and is considered a dietary mistake. In the liver, the fatty acid uptake is high.

At the same time, the -oxidation of fats in the liver decreases (preferred is the alcohol oxidation). Normally, in the liver, the fats provide hydrogen for metabolic processes, and now the alcohol takes over the role of hydrogen donor. Despite a decreased -oxidation, more acetyl-CoA is produced, and this induces ketone body production due to NADH accumulation-induced slowdown of the TCA cycle. In the liver, the fat synthesis increases (among the substrates, there is now more -glycero-phosphate of glycolysis origin), while the apoprotein production is disordered. Due to the increasing free radical (R() production, and the lipid-peroxidation is enhanced (membrane damage, cancerogenic effect). As a result of these processes, the liver becomes fatty, its fat content may increase from the normal 5% even up to 50%, and the resulting steatosis hepatis develops.

In regards to the development of hyperlipoproteinemia (increased risk of atherosclerosis), the apoproteins of an abnormal structure, the consequent decrease of lipoprotein-lipase (LPL) activity, and the elevation of chylomicron, VLDL, IDL levels adjoin. The often described rise in HDL-level affects not the most beneficial HDL2b fraction, but instead, the HDL3 fraction.

Protein

In general, the protein synthesis decreases, yet the hydroxyproline production is enhanced, and this may unfavorably enhance the collagen production in the process of liver cirrhosis.

Purine

The production of uric acid is strongly increased, since, upon accumulation of NADH, the ATP breakdown and the activity of xanthine-oxidase are enhanced. The acidosis induced by lactate and ketone-body molecules enhances the urate reabsorption in the renal tubules, thereby promoting the development of a gout attack.

Porphyrin

Proto- and coproporphyria may develop, eventually with characteristic skin symptoms (e.g. excoriating dermatitis on the face, hands, and areas which come in contact with natural sunlight).

Hormone Metabolism

The metabolism (breakdown) of testosterone increases (estrone-derivatives are produced), while that of the estrogen decreases, causing characteristic feminization among men.

The direct toxic effects of acetaldehyde

The acetaldehyde level is dependent upon the activity of mitochondrial acetaldehyde-dehydrogenase.

The AA, bound to proteins, forms neoantigen. It also appears in the circulation, and is excreted by the kidney. It may evoke an immune reaction.

Since the AA binds to -SH groups of proteins, the tubulin polymerisation is damaged. The microtubular injuries lead to IC retention of proteins (the microtubular breakdown of proteins is damaged). Due to the intracellular accumulation of proteins, and to the steatosis, hepatomegaly develops, the cell volumes may increase up to 2-3-times the normal quantity, thereby compressing the bile duct and the portal vessels (contributing to the portal hypertension induced by perivenular-perisinusoidal fibrosis). The swollen cells may be dysfunctional.

Mitochondrial changes (structural/functional): The swelling of the organelles, and the appearance of abnormal christs, while the fatty acid and acetaldehyde oxidation decreases, the cytochrome and succinyl-dehydrogenase enzyme-activity is decreased, the respiratory capacity is decreased (decreased O2-utilization), and enhanced acetaldehyde-sensitivity can be observed.

Acetaldehyde, by binding to -SH group containing cysteine and glutathione, decreases the number of scavengers. At the same time, using the alcohol-activated MEOS and NADPH in the presence of O2, results in R( production. Increasingly more H2O2 is produced, and the lipid-peroxidation is enhanced. H2O2 may be derived from other reactions (xanthine-oxidation, neutrophils). The ferritin Fe3+ ( Fe2+ transformation with the aid of NADH characterizes the beginning of lipid-peroxidation (after alcohol consumption, the liver non-hem iron and the serum ferritin content rise).

The AA inhibits the repair-enzymes of alkylated nucleoproteins (co-carcinogenic effect).

Effects of acetate:

Upon the effect of acidosis, the cardiac output decreases, including the myocardial contractility, and coronary perfusion.

The acetate inhibits the lipolysis, and aids in the development of steatosis. It promotes the ATP-breakdown, of which, enhances purine production, the uric acid level increases, and this promotes the development of gout.

METABOLIC, TOXIC EFFECTS OF ALCOHOL INDEPENDENT OF ADH ACTIVITY

In the case of acute alcohol consumption, the ethanol competes with other pharmacons (xenobiotics) in support of binding to the cytochrome-P450 system (thereby causes actually incalculable degree of higher pharmacon level, a stronger pharmacon effect, e.g. sedatives, narcotics (Fig. A5.1.). In the case of permanent chronic consumption, it promotes the production of P450 enzymes, speeds up the drug clearance, e.g. the effect of oral anticoncipients may become insufficient, and during surgery, the effect of narcotics may be insufficient, while certain toxic drug-metabolites, toxins may accumulate, e.g. halothane, acetaminophen, phenylbutazone, or the procarcinogenic aflatoxin, CCl4 and N-nitrosodimethylamine reactive metabolites, during surgery, the effect of narcotics may be insufficient.

The transformation of steroid hormones is altered, and feminization may develop.

Both A and D vitamins are substrates of the microsomal enzyme system. Increased microsomal activity leads to depletion of vitamin A, and production of more toxic intermediary substances.

Utilization of NADH without ATP production infers in which all energy is transformed to heat, of which, eventually may lead to the loss of body weight and energy. Calorie intake in the form of alcohol refers only to “empty calories”, of which, serves to increase the body weight (beer-belly).

SYSTEMIC EFFECTS OF ACUTE/CHRONIC ALCOHOL CONSUMPTION

Body weight:

In the beginning, with normal food intake, the body weight increases, however, severe chronic alcoholics become cachectic.

.

Bone, Ca, Mg, PO4:

Osteoporosis (metabolic origin /increased protein catabolism, acidic metabolites/) and muscle damage develops.

Endocrine changes:

The estrogen clearance decreases, the testosterone breakdown increases, feminization, the elevation in prolactin levels, hypogonadism, gynecomastia, pseudo-Cushing, the decrease in the GH level (particularly following alcohol consumption in the evening), and the stress-induced rise of arginine-vasopressin secretion may all be observed. The TSH-response to TRH is insufficient, however, hypothyroidism is not usual. In chronic pancreatitis among chronic alcoholics, both the insulin and the glucagon production decreases. Comprehensively, a secondary form of DM develops, which resembles 1DM.

Vitamin-, trace element-  and antioxidant-deficiency:

Due to the transformation of the A-vitamin-storing stellate cells (Ito cells), and the rapidly increased vitamin metabolism regarding A-vitamin, due to the increased catabolism D vitamin, due to the higher need and/or decreased intake B vitamin-complex deficiency may be expected. The storage of B12 vitamin is now decreased. The decrease of antioxidant activity is often observed, and, in in the background, in addition to the SH-binding nature of acetaldehyde and enhanced production of free radicals, vitamin deficiencies can often be found. Among irregularly fed alcoholics, trace element deficiencies may also occur.

Gastrointestinal system:

In the stomach, alcohol may induce gastritis, peptic ulcer and/or decreased peristaltic movements. Esophageal tumors are more frequent among alcoholics. In the background of maldigestion, defective mucosal enzyme activity, chronic pancreatitis and decreased pancreatic juices emptying (higher tonality of the Oddi sphincter) may be detected. The lack of bile, villus atrophy and portal congestion leads to malabsorption. Frequently, recurrent acute inflammation in chronic pancreatitis is commonly seen. Real acute pancreatitis is characteristic, specifically regarding those individuals who are not used to alcohol consumption, but occasionally experience and undergo massive alcohol intoxication.

Liver:

Hepatomegaly, steatosis hepatis and hepatitis occurs more frequently among alcoholics. In regards to the various severe chronic complications, the hepatic cirrhosis, portal hypertension, the opening of portocaval shunts, or the rupture of esophageal-varix with life-threatening bleeding all are potential risk factors. In some cases, the liver failure is accompanied with hepatorenal syndrome and its consequences. The hepatic injury is most pronounced in the centrilobular region of the liver (the pO2 is lowest here). The portal venules may also be injured due to increased level of local ADH activity. Lactate and acetaldehyde promote the transformation of Ito cells (storing vitamin A and lipids) to myofibroblasts, which are equally suitable regarding multiplication and collagen production (cirrhosis).

Cardiovascular system:

Alcohol, acetaldehyde, and formerly, cobalt found in beer all play a role in the development of secondary dilative cardiomyopathy. The occurrence of arrhythmias is more frequent in the damaged myocardium.

Although acutely the alcohol has a vasodilator effect, in the long run, it enhances the secretion of some stress hormones, e.g. arginine-vasopressin, or noradrenaline, thus, chronic alcoholism tends to cause hypertension.

An effect of alcohol causing coronary spasm has been observed, however, in regards to wine, may have cardioprotective effect, which is referred to as the, “French paradox”, (Fig. A5.3.). The protective effect may be ascribed partly to the polyphenols (which contribute to the color of the wine), partly to the alcohol which, in its role as a toxin, stimulates mechanisms of prevention. The latter naturally holds true only in the very moderate consumption of red wine and of lowered alcohol content. For obvious reasons, individuals are not advised nor encouraged to start drinking wine strictly in response to this scientific finding, however, the former, usual, cultured consumption of good quality red wine is no longer strictly prohibited.

Hemopoietic system:

The toxic effects of alcohol cause depression to the bone marrow, anemia, and is an anticoagulant effect, of which, proves advantageous regarding AMI. Due to unknown mechanisms, the size of red blood cells increases.

Immune system:

The function of polymorphonuclear cells decreases, including the lymphocyte proliferation, and the inhibition of cellular immunity increases the tendency for infections.

Tumor:

Malignant processes develop primarily in the liver, the esophagus, the nasopharynx, the larynx, and also in the distal part of the GI-tract.

Thermoregulation:

By disabling mechanisms in support of the central nervous system, alcohol suppresses the prevention mechanisms against both cold and warmth. The membrane fluidity increases, the heat sensation increases, and peripheral vasodilation may even decrease the perception of cold. Under the effects of alcohol, the peril of hypothermia is pronounced. From another perspective, in a warm environment, heat stroke easily develops.

Nervous system:

In acute effects of alcohol, apart from disorders of regulations (vasodilation, body temperature and nausea) the behavior and mood are altered (increase of membrane fluidity has a sedative, anti-anxiety effect), including consciousness and judgement. Alcohol enhances the production of the inhibitor -amino-butyric acid (GABA) neurotransmitter (the GABA-receptor also binds barbiturates, benzodiazepines), and enhances the chloride-inflow of GABA-receptor. The glycine-neurotransmission may similarly be affected. Alcohol consumption inhibits the excitatory glutamate neurotransmitter (on N-methyl-D-aspartate, i.e. NMDA-receptors), it influences certain secondary messenger systems, and modifies the removal of dopamine (and serotonin). The serotonin level increases. The reaction time becomes longer, therefore, the risk of automobile accidents is significantly higher.

Among chronic alcoholics, encephalopathies, cerebral atrophy, dementia, peripheral neuropathies similar as those in diabetes mellitus, uremia, (sensory, motor, autonomic) may develop (Wernicke encephalopathy and Korsakoff syndrome). Black-outs (complete loss of memory, often hidden by confabulation of the anxious patient), are frequent, accompanied with severe cognitive and affective (sentimental and emotional) disorders develop. The learning, memory, and other intellectual performance types (e.g. creativity) decreases, and emotional eruptions and lasting, deep depression periods are frequently reported.  Among alcoholics, suicide is the 3rd most frequent cause of death. Chronic alcoholism significantly increases the risk of paranoid psychosis, often leading to senseless murder. Tolerance and physical dependence develops. In the transmitter systems, in the adaptation of transmitter systems, the arginine-vasopressin production is at work, NMDA-upregulation develops, and the intrinsic serotonin production decreases. The mechanisms responsible for the development of the symptoms in support of alcohol-withdrawal have not yet been identified. The cells do not adapt to the toxic effects of ethanol or acetaldehyde.

Fetal alcohol syndrome:

Maternal alcohol consumption during pregnancy causes severe disorders in the fetal development. Placental dysfunction, nutritive disorders, eventually hypoglycemia and ketoacidosis, and fetal ischemia due to collapse of umbilical vessels, toxic and teratogenic acetaldehyde crossing the placenta, including the enhanced PG-synthesis all contribute to growth retardation, microcephaly, craniofacial abnormalities, cognitive and behavioral disorders observed in neonates of alcoholic mothers.

Alcohol-induced epigenetic disorders

According to recently published animal experiments, chronic alcohol consumption causes epigenetic mutations in the genetic material (e.g. in the sperm of male animals), which possibly contributes to the development of alcohol-problems in future generations of their offspring. The epigenetic alterations of the brain may play a role in the development of alcohol-related behavioral disorders.

PRINCIPLES IN THE TREATMENT OF ALCOHOL ADDICTION

The acute symptoms of deprivation (trembling, sweating, agitation and nausea) may be somewhat lessened by the sedative pharmacon (e.g. Tiapridal).

Physical alcohol-dependence may be moderated in the clinical administration of serotonin agonist pharmacon.

To evoke a conditioned aversion, the inhibitor of acetaldehyde breakdown (disulfiram) or opiate antagonist (naltrexone) is preferred, and both elicit severe malaise. Nausea/malaise accompanying a new taste leads to aversion. This may be similar among humans, however, the result is doubtful as seen in in a well-known taste such as alcohol.
